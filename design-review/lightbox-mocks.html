Lightbox mocks

Three directions for the artwork viewer, each shown on a laptop and a phone and drawn in the site's own palette. The frames run live: click a thumbnail to open, then use the arrow keys, Escape, double-click to zoom, or swipe inside a phone frame.

Placeholder artwork drawn in SVG. Type, colors, and surfaces match the site.

A

Quiet viewer

Keeps the paper surface and the current scale-in, then fixes the mechanics. The image grows out of the thumbnail you clicked, arrows preload their neighbors, a counter marks your place, and double-click zooms in for detail. On a phone it turns gesture-first: swipe to move, swipe down to dismiss. This is the version that changes the least on a desktop.

B

Paper room

Uses the same paper surface as the page, but lets the image take over. Chrome withdraws after a couple of seconds and returns when you move, the image sits larger than in A, and a filmstrip along the bottom shows the set and where you are in it. This is the immersive direction that stays inside the site's palette.

C

Gallery plate

When a piece needs a label, give it one. On a desktop the image shares the screen with a plate: category, title, medium, dimensions, year, and the set as a list you can jump through, with labeled previous and next. On a phone the image runs edge to edge and the plate becomes a sheet you drag up from the bottom.

What the real build needs

  • Open from the thumbnail with a View Transition instead of the JavaScript FLIP used in the mock. The gallery already ships View Transitions for the filters.
  • Preload the next and previous images once the viewer opens, and keep the existing decode() loading spinner.
  • Dismiss on a swipe down past 90px or a flick faster than 0.11px/ms, with damping when the drag direction changes.
  • Zoom on double-tap and ctrl+wheel, clamp panning to the image edges, and reset zoom when the image changes.
  • In the paper room, hide chrome after 2.5s idle, restore it on pointer or key, and never hide it while zoomed.
  • Keep focus inside the viewer, return it to the thumbnail on close, and drop all transforms under prefers-reduced-motion.
